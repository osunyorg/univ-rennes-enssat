About ENSSAT summary: >-

ENSSAT (École Nationale Supérieure des Sciences Appliquées et de Technologie) trains engineers in computer science (cybersecurity, artificial intelligence, embedded systems) and photonics - two strategic fields in which our graduates find employment in less than two months. A public school of the University of Rennes, affiliated with the Institut Mines-Télécom, it is located in the heart of one of France’s leading tech hubs, in Lannion - a technological ecosystem unique in France where Orange, Nokia, Thales, and Lumibird recruit just minutes from the school and the sea.

Our digital DNA

We train engineers in complementary fields: •    Computer Science (cybersecurity, artificial intelligence, embedded systems, connected devices, software development) •    Photonics (laser technologies, biophotonics, quantum photonics, telecommunications) Each program - whether as a full-time student or an apprentice - combines academic excellence, hands-on projects, and immersion in the workplace starting in the first year. As a result, our graduates are hired, on average, even before they receive their degrees.

A school born of a pioneering vision

In the 1960s, the French telecommunications industry was born right here! Enssat was founded to meet a very specific need: when CNET (now Orange Innovation) set up shop in Lannion in the 1960s, Northern Brittany needed engineers trained locally. The school was founded in 1986 to meet this demand - and this close connection to the industry has continued ever since. It’s no coincidence that Orange, Nokia, Thales , Lumibird, and dozens of innovative startups have their teams just minutes away from our classrooms. Our students interact with these professionals on a daily basis: during internships, on projects, and sometimes even in the cafeteria of the neighboring lab.

History

1959
Under the influence of Pierre Marzin, the city experienced significant growth in the telecommunications sector following the relocation of the Cnet (National Center for Telecommunications Research) from Issy-les-Moulineaux to Lannion.

1962
On July 11, 1962, the Radôme in Pleumeur-Bodou made a world first possible: signals transmitted by the twin station in Andover, United States, and relayed by the Telstar 1 satellite, established the first intercontinental television link.

1985
"Mr. Mayor, you will have your engineering school" , François Mitterrand - French President from 1981 to 1995 - officially announced to Yves Nédelec, mayor of Lannion.

1986
March 17: Publication in the Official Journal of the decree establishing ENSSAT. This primarily local initiative was spearheaded by Jacques Wolf (professor of mathematics at the Lannion University Institute of Technology). He would become the school’s first director upon its founding in 1986. At that time, Paul Gourtay (director of Alcatel), Jacques Papet-Lépine (in charge of promoting scientific development in Brittany at the CNET), and Jean-Claude Collet (director general of services at Lannion City Hall) also supported this initiative.

An exceptional living environment

Studying at Enssat also means living just a stone’s throw from the Pink Granite Coast: beaches, hiking trails, surfing and sailing spots, and charming villages. Students get involved in campus life through existing clubs (examples: film club, Enssaltitude, table tennis, holography, gaming, visual arts, music...) and events such as the WEI or the Gala. Added to this are water sports and a variety of other activities (river or sea kayaking, tennis, rugby, boxing, kendo, basketball, golf, etc.), which are mandatory in the first year, as well as a vibrant community life in Lannion. A rare balance between professional ambition and quality of life, without the pressure or cost of living in a major metropolis—yet connected to the entire world through the technologies invented right here.

A research center

Research at ENSSAT is integral to the curriculum: the faculty members who lead the seven research teams at the CNRS, IRISA (INRIA), IETR, and Institut Foton laboratories are also the ones who teach the courses. Our students nearing the end of their studies can complete an internship, pursue a master’s degree, or continue on to a doctoral program there.

A School Open to the World

As an engineering school, ENSSAT promotes exchanges to develop its international and corporate relationships. To date, 52 international agreements - both within and outside Europe - have been signed, allowing every student to have a rewarding experience in another country. In fact, this is a recommendation from the CTI: 100% of engineering students must complete an international experience (academic exchange or internship). Partnerships with the economic and industrial ecosystems of western France enable our students to join companies for both internships and jobs.

Grande École d'ingénieur·es

ENSSAT is a public school accredited by the Ministry of Higher Education, Research, and Space following a review by the CTI . It is part of the University of Rennes and is an “ affiliated school of the Institut Mines-Télécom " . GOOD TO KNOW: The CTI’s EUR-ACE accreditation, reserved for schools that have obtained the maximum accreditation period, guarantees that our graduates’ engineering degrees are recognized throughout Europe.
